# Accessibility and usable interaction controls 61-80

[Standard index](../website-engineering-standard.md) | [Previous module](controls-046-060-css.md) | [Next module](controls-081-095-security.md)

## Controls

61. WCAG 2.2 Level AA is the engineering baseline unless a stricter contractual or legal requirement applies.
62. Accessibility is implemented in native structure, content, and interaction; an overlay or widget is never accepted as a substitute for remediation.
63. All navigation, controls, dialogs, menus, forms, and media can be operated with a keyboard alone.
64. A visible-on-focus skip link reaches the main content, and repeated blocks can be bypassed efficiently.
65. Landmarks and accessible names let assistive-technology users identify navigation, search, main content, complementary content, and footer regions.
66. Interactive components expose correct accessible names, roles, values, states, descriptions, and status changes.
67. Focus order follows the meaningful reading and interaction sequence, and focus is restored or moved intentionally after dynamic actions.
68. Content remains usable at 200 percent text zoom and through responsive reflow equivalent to 400 percent zoom at standard desktop width.
69. Text, large text, controls, focus indicators, charts, and meaningful graphics meet applicable contrast requirements.
70. Color, position, shape, sound, or animation is not the sole way to communicate meaning, state, validation, or required action.
71. Informative images have concise purpose-based alternative text rather than filenames, keyword stuffing, or redundant visible captions.
72. Decorative images use empty alternative text or an equivalent mechanism so assistive technology can ignore them.
73. Form fields have persistent labels, instructions, required-state communication, appropriate autocomplete values, and accessible grouping.
74. Validation identifies errors in text, associates messages with fields, preserves input, moves focus appropriately, and announces status.
75. Interactive targets provide sufficient size and spacing, including on touch screens and at responsive breakpoints.
76. Navigation, labels, component behavior, and help mechanisms remain consistent across templates and processes.
77. Prerecorded media includes accurate captions and necessary transcripts or audio description; autoplay is avoided.
78. Dialogs, disclosures, carousels, menus, accordions, and tabs follow established accessible interaction patterns or are replaced with simpler native controls.
79. Release testing includes manual keyboard, zoom, high-contrast or forced-color, and screen-reader checks on representative complete processes.
80. The site provides an accurate accessibility statement and contact path without making an unsupported conformance or legal-compliance claim.
